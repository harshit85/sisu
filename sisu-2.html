The Bilona method

Nothing about Sisu is rushed

Sisu started in Kanpur with one idea: ghee should be made the way it always was — by hand, in small batches, with nothing added to speed it up or stretch it out.

01

Milk, sourced daily

Fresh A2 cow and buffalo milk collected each morning from farms we know by name.

02

Curd, set overnight

The milk is set into curd using natural culture and left to develop slowly overnight.

03

Hand-churned to butter

The curd is churned by hand with a wooden bilona in small batches — this is the step that gives the method its name, and it can't be rushed by a machine.

04

Simmered low, until golden

The butter is cooked on a low flame until the milk solids settle and the ghee turns golden and fragrant.

05

Jarred, without additives

Cooled, strained, and jarred as-is — no preservatives, no added colour, no shortcuts.

Questions

Good to know before you order

What makes Bilona ghee different from regular ghee?

Most ghee is made by heating cream or butter directly. Bilona ghee is made by first setting milk into curd, hand-churning that curd into butter, and only then simmering it slowly — a slower, more traditional process that many people feel gives a richer aroma and flavour.

Legacy or Heritage — which one should I pick?

Legacy (cow milk) is lighter and grassy, good for everyday cooking. Heritage (buffalo milk) is richer and deeper in colour, and goes further in parathas, sweets and festive dishes. If you're unsure, tell us what you mostly cook and we'll help you choose over WhatsApp.

How should I store the ghee, and how long does it keep?

Keep the jar tightly closed in a cool, dry place away from direct sunlight and moisture — no refrigeration needed. Always use a clean, dry spoon so no water gets into the jar, since moisture is what causes ghee to spoil faster.
